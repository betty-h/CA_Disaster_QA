# Evaluation Method
## CA Disaster Recovery Chatbot — Q&A Dataset

---

## 1. Approach: LLM-as-Judge

Answers are evaluated using an **LLM-as-judge** framework implemented in `evaluate_qa.py`. Rather than relying on token-overlap metrics (BLEU, ROUGE) — which are poor proxies for answer quality in open-ended conversational tasks — a Claude Opus model is used as the judge. For each question, the judge receives the question, a gold-standard reference answer, and the candidate answer, and scores the candidate on six independent dimensions using a 1–5 integer scale.

This approach is well-suited to disaster recovery chatbot evaluation because the answers are short, factual, and actionable — qualities that surface-form similarity metrics cannot reliably measure. A candidate answer that correctly names FEMA's phone number but leaves out the registration URL scores differently from one that inverts those facts, and a token-overlap metric would not distinguish the two.

---

## 2. Inputs

### 2.1 Gold answers

The reference set is `qa_pairs.json`, produced by `generate_qa_pairs.py`. Each gold pair has:

```json
{
  "question": "can i apply for fema and sba at the same time",
  "answer":   "Yes, and you should. Register with FEMA first at DisasterAssistance.gov ...",
  "topic":    "FEMA registration and individual assistance",
  "tier":     "tier_1_critical",
  "style":    "terse"
}
```

The gold answers were generated by Claude Opus grounded in scraped content from 28 authoritative sources (FEMA, CalOES, SBA, EDD, CA DOI, etc.) and are treated as the reference standard for factual accuracy and completeness.

### 2.2 Candidate predictions

The file to evaluate is `predictions.jsonl` — one JSON object per line, each containing the question text and the model's answer:

```jsonl
{"question": "can i apply for fema and sba at the same time", "answer": "..."}
{"question": "how long does FEMA aproval take", "answer": "..."}
```

The `question` field is matched to the gold set by exact text (case-insensitive). Unmatched questions are skipped and counted as lookup failures.

---

## 3. Rubric

Each candidate answer is scored on six dimensions. Scores are integers 1–5. The **weighted score** is Σ(raw score × weight) across all six dimensions, producing a single composite value between **1.0 and 5.0**.

```
weighted_score = (factual_accuracy × 0.30)
               + (completeness     × 0.20)
               + (actionability    × 0.20)
               + (tone             × 0.10)
               + (conciseness      × 0.10)
               + (hallucination    × 0.10)
```

### Dimension 1 — Factual Accuracy (weight: 30%)

> Are the facts, program names, eligibility rules, phone numbers, and URLs in the evaluated answer correct relative to the gold answer and general knowledge of CA/federal disaster recovery programs?

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1 | Multiple factual errors (wrong agency, wrong number, wrong rules) |
| 2 | One significant factual error |
| 3 | Mostly correct, one minor slip |
| 4 | Correct with very minor imprecision |
| 5 | Fully accurate |

Factual accuracy carries the highest weight (30%) because incorrect information in a disaster context carries direct harm. A displaced person directed to the wrong agency or given a wrong phone number faces real consequences — this failure mode is treated as the most serious.

---

### Dimension 2 — Completeness (weight: 20%)

> Does the evaluated answer cover the core informational needs expressed in the question and present in the gold answer? A complete answer need not be exhaustive — it must not omit the most critical point(s).

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1 | Misses the main point of the question entirely |
| 2 | Addresses the topic but omits the key piece of information |
| 3 | Covers the main point; misses one or more secondary points |
| 4 | Covers nearly everything; minor omission only |
| 5 | Fully addresses the question |

This dimension distinguishes answers that are accurate-but-incomplete from answers that are genuinely sufficient. A technically correct statement that omits the most actionable part of the answer ("yes, FEMA assistance exists" without saying how to apply) scores low on completeness.

---

### Dimension 3 — Actionability (weight: 20%)

> Does the answer give the user at least one clear, concrete next step — a URL to visit, a number to call, a form to file? Disaster victims need to know what to DO, not just what exists.

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1 | No actionable guidance whatsoever |
| 2 | Implies action exists but provides no concrete step |
| 3 | Vague guidance ("contact FEMA" without how) |
| 4 | One clear next step, could be more specific |
| 5 | Clear, specific next step(s) with URL or phone number |

Actionability is weighted equally with completeness (20%) because in a crisis context, knowing what exists is less useful than knowing what to do. An answer that names the right programme but omits the application URL or phone number partially fails the user.

---

### Dimension 4 — Tone & Empathy (weight: 10%)

> Is the tone appropriate for a stressed, possibly grieving disaster victim? It should be warm but efficient — not cold or bureaucratic, not excessively verbose, and not dismissive. Penalise clinical jargon without explanation and unnecessary caveats that delay the useful information.

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1 | Cold, dismissive, or filled with unexplained bureaucratic jargon |
| 2 | Noticeably impersonal or alienating |
| 3 | Neutral — neither warm nor cold |
| 4 | Warm and approachable, minor issues |
| 5 | Warm, clear, and efficient — appropriately human without being saccharine |

Tone is weighted lower (10%) than accuracy and completeness, reflecting the priority ordering: a warm but inaccurate answer is worse than a terse but correct one. However, tone failure is still meaningful — a chatbot that responds to "I lost everything in the fire" with robotic bureaucratic language fails users in a different but real way.

---

### Dimension 5 — Conciseness (weight: 10%)

> Is the response an appropriate length for a chatbot reply? Target: 2–6 sentences. Penalise unnecessary padding, repeated information, or walls of text. Also penalise answers so short they omit essential information.

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1 | Far too long (essay-length) or far too short (single sentence missing key content) |
| 2 | Notably padded or truncated |
| 3 | Acceptable but could be tighter or fuller |
| 4 | Good length, minor inefficiency |
| 5 | Ideal chatbot length — complete but not padded |

The target length of 2–6 sentences reflects chatbot UX norms. Note that a very short answer that omits critical content would be penalised on both Completeness and Conciseness; these are not redundant because Completeness asks *what* is covered and Conciseness asks *how much space* is used.

---

### Dimension 6 — Hallucination / Inverse (weight: 10%)

> Does the answer introduce specific claims that are NOT in the gold answer AND are not verifiable general knowledge — e.g., invented dollar amounts, fabricated programme names, made-up deadlines?

**This dimension is scored inversely: 5 means no hallucinations.**

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1 | Clear fabrications — invented numbers, fake programmes, false deadlines |
| 2 | One claim that is likely fabricated |
| 3 | One uncertain or unverifiable claim, possible hallucination |
| 4 | No hallucinations but one claim that is slightly imprecise |
| 5 | No hallucinations — every specific claim is verifiable |

Hallucination is scored separately from Factual Accuracy to distinguish two failure types: *errors of commission* (hallucinated facts not in the gold answer) versus *errors of omission or distortion* (getting real facts wrong). Both are penalised, but they have different diagnostic implications for a model under evaluation.

---

## 4. Judge Prompt

The judge is given the following inputs in a single API call:

```
[System]
You are an expert evaluator for a California disaster recovery chatbot dataset.
Your job is to score a candidate answer against a gold-standard answer using a
provided rubric. You must be rigorous and consistent.
Always return valid JSON and nothing else.

[User]
## Question
{question}

## Gold Answer (reference)
{gold_answer}

## Candidate Answer (to evaluate)
{candidate_answer}

## Rubric
{rubric_block}   ← all six dimensions with descriptions and scales

## Instructions
Score the candidate answer on each dimension (integer 1–5).
For each dimension also write a 1-sentence rationale.
Return ONLY a JSON object with this exact structure:
{
  "scores": {
    "<dimension_key>": {
      "score": <int 1–5>,
      "rationale": "<one sentence>"
    }, ...
  },
  "overall_notes": "<optional 1–2 sentence summary>"
}
```

The judge is instructed to return structured JSON only. The evaluator validates the response — checking that all six keys are present and all scores are integers in [1, 5] — and retries up to three times with exponential backoff (2s, 4s) on malformed output or API errors.

---

## 5. Scoring Formula

```python
weighted_score = sum(score[dim] * weight[dim] for dim in DIMENSIONS)
```

With all six dimensions scoring 5, the maximum weighted score is **5.0**. With all scoring 1, the minimum is **1.0**.

### Example

| Dimension | Weight | Score | Contribution |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Factual Accuracy | 0.30 | 5 | 1.50 |
| Completeness | 0.20 | 4 | 0.80 |
| Actionability | 0.20 | 5 | 1.00 |
| Tone & Empathy | 0.10 | 4 | 0.40 |
| Conciseness | 0.10 | 5 | 0.50 |
| Hallucination | 0.10 | 5 | 0.50 |
| **Weighted total** | | | **4.70** |

---

## 6. Aggregation and Reporting

After all pairs are scored, the evaluator computes aggregate statistics and saves two output files.

### 6.1 Breakdowns

Results are aggregated along three axes:

- **Overall** — mean weighted score and per-dimension means across all evaluated pairs
- **By tier** — mean weighted score per topic tier (critical, high-volume, moderate, long-tail). Models often degrade on long-tail topics where training data is sparse.
- **By question style** — mean weighted score per writing style (terse, grammatical, typo, etc.). Models may handle well-formed grammatical questions better than terse or typo-laden ones.

### 6.2 Output files

| File | Contents |
|---|---|
| `eval_results.json` | Per-pair scores with rationales + aggregate statistics |
| `eval_report.md` | Human-readable summary with tier/style tables and bottom-10 pairs |

### 6.3 Per-pair record structure (`eval_results.json`)

```json
{
  "question":         "how long does FEMA aproval take",
  "gold_answer":      "FEMA typically inspects within 10 days ...",
  "candidate_answer": "FEMA approval usually takes 2–3 weeks ...",
  "topic":            "FEMA registration and individual assistance",
  "tier":             "tier_1_critical",
  "style":            "typo",
  "weighted_score":   3.9,
  "eval": {
    "scores": {
      "factual_accuracy":  { "score": 4, "rationale": "Timeframe is approximate but not wrong." },
      "completeness":      { "score": 3, "rationale": "Misses the inspection step." },
      "actionability":     { "score": 4, "rationale": "Suggests checking status online." },
      "tone":              { "score": 4, "rationale": "Warm and clear." },
      "conciseness":       { "score": 5, "rationale": "Two sentences, well-sized." },
      "hallucination":     { "score": 4, "rationale": "One slightly imprecise figure." }
    },
    "overall_notes": "Solid answer; main gap is omitting the home inspection process."
  }
}
```

## 7. Ceiling Benchmark (Self-Eval)

Running `--self-eval` scores the gold answers against themselves — the same answer appears as both gold and candidate. This functions as a **ceiling check**: a well-formed gold answer evaluated against itself should score 4.5–5.0 on every dimension.

```bash
python3 evaluate_qa.py --self-eval --max-pairs 10
```

If the self-eval mean falls significantly below 4.5, it indicates a problem with the gold answer quality or the judge prompt rather than with the model under test. The self-eval score should be computed before interpreting any model evaluation results and reported alongside them as a reference ceiling.

## 8. Usage

```bash
export ANTHROPIC_API_KEY=sk-ant-...

# Evaluate a model's predictions
python3 evaluate_qa.py --predictions predictions.jsonl

# Evaluate with a custom output prefix
python3 evaluate_qa.py --predictions predictions.jsonl --output-prefix run1

# Ceiling check on 10 pairs
python3 evaluate_qa.py --self-eval --max-pairs 10

# Use a different judge model
python3 evaluate_qa.py --predictions predictions.jsonl --model claude-sonnet-4-6
```

### CLI flags

| Flag | Default | Description |
|---|---|---|
| `--gold` | `qa_pairs.json` | Gold Q&A pairs |
| `--predictions` | — | JSONL file of model outputs to evaluate |
| `--self-eval` | — | Score gold answers against themselves |
| `--output-prefix` | `eval` | Prefix for `_results.json` and `_report.md` |
| `--model` | `claude-opus-4-7` | Judge model |
| `--delay` | `0.5` | Seconds between API calls |
| `--max-pairs` | all | Limit to first N pairs (for smoke tests) |

## 9. Design Decisions and Limitations

**Why not BLEU/ROUGE?** Token-overlap metrics are poorly suited to short factual answers with multiple correct phrasings. "Call 1-800-621-3362" and "dial FEMA's helpline at 1-800-621-3362" have low overlap but are semantically equivalent. Conversely, an answer that copies irrelevant text from the gold answer scores falsely high on overlap metrics.

**Why use gold answers as the reference rather than raw source documents?** The gold answers are concise, chatbot-appropriate distillations of the scraped content. Using the raw source pages as reference would introduce noise — most pages contain navigation text, legal disclaimers, and tangential information. The gold answer defines what a correct chatbot response looks like, not what the original document says.

**Judge consistency.** LLM judges can be inconsistent across runs. To mitigate this, the judge is given precise integer scale anchors for each dimension and instructed to be rigorous and consistent. For production use, running two independent judge calls per pair and averaging (or flagging disagreements above a threshold) would improve reliability.

**Hallucination detection is limited.** The hallucination dimension catches confident fabrications but may not flag subtle numerical errors (e.g., a deadline shifted by one day) that fall outside the gold answer's scope. Cross-referencing against the scraped source documents would strengthen this dimension.

**Style-specific expectations.** The rubric does not currently adjust expectations by question style. A terse question ("where do i register for fema") arguably requires a shorter answer than a conversational one, and the Tone dimension might reasonably expect more warmth in response to an emotional question. Future iterations could make these adjustments explicit in the judge prompt.
